# Preset schema

Version 1, 2026-10-31. A preset is one JSON file in the presets folder, and it describes one setting: a floor material pair and a wall material pair, plus the defaults for variants and jitter. This document defines every field, and the twenty-three shipped presets in the presets folder are complete examples: the eleven settings built with the client (lava_cave, greek_ruins, ice_cave, cursed_lands, dungeon, medieval, swamp, temple, enchanted, magic_cave and crystal_cave) and a dozen high fantasy samples authored from the families alone (tavern, forgotten_castle, wizard_tower, elven_hall, dwarven_forge, catacombs, throne_room, dragon_lair, haunted_library, frozen_fortress, sunken_temple and mushroom_grotto).

## Status

Draft. The field names are final once the first preset renders, because settings.json stores a full preset snapshot and renaming a field later would break reproduction of old exports. Until then the names can change freely.

## Numeric parameters and ranges

Every numeric parameter accepts two forms. The short form is a plain number, for example "scale": 0.1, and a parameter in the short form is fixed: it never jitters and never randomizes. The long form is an object with value, min and max, for example "scale": {"value": 0.1, "min": 0.05, "max": 0.2}, and a parameter in the long form takes part in jitter and in randomize parameters, within its range. In practice this means the preset author decides which knobs the variants may move, and a preset with every parameter in the short form produces variants that differ by seed only. The one exception is the layout parameters of the blocks, timber, scatter and crystal families and every field of the columns family, listed with those families, which never take a range because every variant of a set shares the layout they shape along the tile edges, and the loader rejects a range on them.

## Top level

The top level object has these fields.

schemaVersion is the integer 1 and lets the loader reject files from a future version with a clear message.

id is a short identifier in letters, digits and underscores, for example lava_cave, and it is the name stored in settings.json and favorites.

displayName is the name shown in the GUI, for example "Lava Cave".

floor and wall are tile type objects, described next.

variants is the default variant count and must be at least 4.

jitter is the jitter fraction applied to ranged parameters in each variant, as a number from 0 to 1. A value of 0.1 means a variant may move a parameter by up to one tenth of its range in either direction.

## Tile type object

A tile type object has these fields.

base is the material shown where there is no garbage.

garbage is the material shown where the coverage mask is on. It is a full material object with the same fields as base, because at coverage 1 it is the whole tile.

garbageCoverage is the default coverage from 0 to 1, and it is the value the GUI slider starts at. The slider shows it as 0 to 100.

garbageScale is the scale of the coverage mask noise as a fraction of the tile, and garbageSoftness is the width of the blend band around the threshold as a fraction of the mask field's range. Both accept ranges. A jittered value only moves the mask away from the tile edges, because along the edges every variant of a set follows a shared mask built from the authored values, which is what makes garbage continue from one variant to the next (SPEC.md, Garbage). The crossover from the shared mask to the variant's own mask sits about half an authored mask cell from the edge, so a large garbageScale shares a wider strip of every tile.

## Material object

A material object has these fields.

structure is an object whose family field is one of field, cells or pattern, and whose other fields depend on the family, as listed below.

ramp is a list of color stops. Each stop has t from 0 to 1 and color as a hex string such as "#4a1c10". The list must be sorted by t, must start at 0 and end at 1, and colors between stops are interpolated linearly in sRGB. A ramp with one color is allowed and produces a flat material whose only variation comes from tint and detail.

detail is an object with scale, octaves and strength. The detail noise is added to the structure height before the ramp is applied, so strength 0 disables it. All three accept ranges.

tint is the per-cell tint strength from 0 to 1, and it is ignored by the field family because that family has no cells. The tint scales the color after the ramp by 1 plus tint times (c minus 0.5), where c is a value from 0 to 1 drawn from the cell id, so a tint of 0.2 moves a cell by up to a tenth of its brightness, and the border between cells keeps its own color. Scaling the color instead of shifting the height is what lets a one-color ramp still show tint.

contrast remaps the height around 0.5 before the ramp, where 1 is no change.

edgeDarkening darkens pixels near a cell edge from 0 (off) to 1, and it is the only remnant of the bevel seen in the client's samples. It defaults to 0 and is ignored by the field family.

transparency, from 0 to 1 with a default of 0, is read on the garbage material only and lets the base show through wherever the garbage structure has no feature, which is the empty ground of the scatter family and the mortar or border of the cell families (DECISIONS.md ADR-017). At 1 the gaps show the base in full, and between 0 and 1 a haze of the garbage color remains. The field family has no gaps, so transparency changes nothing on it. Transparency never takes a range, because the haze reaches the tile edges. The measured coverage counts the mask and not the painted pixels, so the slider keeps its meaning. A transparent scatter or crystal garbage shows each stone or cluster whole or not at all, by the mask under its center, and only the haze between them follows the mask per pixel (DECISIONS.md ADR-019), so garbageSoftness shapes the haze alone there.

cellColors is a boolean with a default of false. When true, the ramp is read at the cell's random value instead of the height, so every cell takes its own color from the ramp and the height sets its brightness from half to full, which gives sparkles in many colors or a wall of mixed stones. The ground and the mortar between cells then take the ramp's color at t 0 whatever their height, so the first stop is the ground color and the rest of the ramp is the palette of the cells. The field family has no cells and ignores it. The darkening is full at the border and fades out over a quarter of a cell into the interior, and it leaves the border or mortar itself alone.

## Structure families

The field family has scale (the size of one lattice cell of the first octave as a fraction of the tile), octaves (an integer from 1 to 8), gain (the amplitude ratio between octaves, usually 0.5), warp (the domain warp strength from 0 to 1), and ridged (a boolean that folds the noise into ridges, which is how lava cracks and crystal veins are made). The number of lattice cells per tile is 1 divided by scale and must be an integer, so the loader rounds it and reports the rounded value.

The cells family has count (the integer number of cells per axis per tile), jitter (how far a cell center may move from its lattice position, from 0 to 1), edgeWidth (the width of the border between cells as a fraction of the tile), and edgeHeight (the height value assigned to the border, usually 0 so that borders map to the dark end of the ramp). The border is centered on the line between two cells, so each cell gives up half of edgeWidth. The cell interior height rises from edgeHeight at the border to 1 at half a lattice cell from it, with a profile that is steep at the border and flat toward the center, so a cell reads as a rounded stone, and the detail noise adds grain on top.

The pattern family has rows (the integer number of rows per tile), columns (the integer number of cells per row), rowOffset (how far every second row is shifted, as a fraction of a cell width, 0.5 for bricks), mortarWidth (as a fraction of the tile), and mortarHeight (the height value of the mortar, usually 0). The mortar is centered on the cell boundary, so each cell gives up half of mortarWidth. The cell interior height is 1 minus a per-cell variation of up to 0.1, and the detail noise adds grain on top.

The blocks family has rows (the integer number of courses per tile), columns (the integer average number of blocks per course), sizeVariation (from 0 to 1, how far course heights and block widths move away from uniform, where 1 spans a factor of nine between the smallest and the largest), tallChance (from 0 to 1, the share of blocks that extend into the next course), mortarWidth and mortarHeight (as in the pattern family) and cornerRadius (as a fraction of the tile, rounding every block corner up to half the block). Rows, columns, sizeVariation and tallChance take no range, because the course heights and the one block per course that crosses the tile's vertical edge are shared by every variant of a set (DECISIONS.md ADR-013), and the GUI shows these rows without a lock. The block interior is flat apart from a small per block height variation, so use edgeDarkening for rounded stones, cornerRadius for worn corners and detail for grain. Keep tint and edgeDarkening without a range in a blocks material, because a variant that moved them would show a brightness step across the block that a tile seam cuts.

The timber family draws a half-timbered wall or a plank floor. It has rows and columns (the integer number of panels per tile each way; the posts and rails of the frame sit on the panel boundaries, so the tile edges run through the middle of a post and a rail), beamWidth (the width of posts and rails as a fraction of the tile, where 0 removes the frame), braceChance (from 0 to 1, the share of panels with a diagonal brace, drawn per variant and ignored without a frame), grain (from 0 to 1, how strongly the wood streaks move the height around the beam height), pegSize (the diameter of the pegs on the posts beside every rail as a fraction of the tile, where 0 removes them), planks (true fills the panels with boards instead of plaster) and plankWidth (the width of one board as a fraction of the tile, rounded so that boards fill a panel exactly). Beams sit at height 0.25 plus or minus a quarter of grain, pegs a little above that, plaster at height 1 and boards at 0.85, each less a small per piece variation, so a preset puts its wood colors between t 0 and 0.45 and its panel colors between t 0.6 and 1. Boards have a groove between them, some carry a knot, and some have two plank ends with a middle piece of its own tint. The frame is shared by every variant of a set (DECISIONS.md ADR-014), so every parameter except braceChance takes no range, and a frameless plank material shares its boards as well, with only its plank ends and knots differing between variants. Keep tint and edgeDarkening without a range here for the reason given for blocks. The booleans ridged, planks and cellColors have no GUI control and are set in the preset file.

The scatter family draws rounded stones, mounds or pebbles of varied size on a ground. It has count (the integer number of lattice cells per tile, with at most one stone per cell), density (from 0 to 1, the share of cells that hold a stone), size (the stone radius in cells, from 0.05 to 1, where 0.5 makes a stone as wide as its cell), sizeVariation (from 0 to 1, where 1 moves a radius by up to 60 percent either way), jitter (from 0 to 1, how far a stone may leave the center of its cell), layers (an integer from 1 to 3, where each further layer adds cells half the size with stones half the size and three quarters of the density), groundHeight (the height of the ground between stones, usually 0), warp (from 0 to 1, how far the lookup bends, where 1 moves a pixel by half a coarse cell and turns the discs into blobs) and stars (from 0 to 1, the share of stones drawn as four point stars with the ramp's end color instead of domes, for glints). A stone is a dome that rises fast at its rim and flattens at the top, overlapping stones show the taller one, and the ground is the edge of this family, so tint and edge darkening apply to the stones only. The stones that reach a tile edge are shared by every variant of a set and use the authored density, size and sizeVariation (DECISIONS.md ADR-015), so these three may range and then reach only the stones inside the tile, while count, jitter, layers, groundHeight, warp and stars never range. Keep tint, edgeDarkening and detail strength without a range here for the reason given for blocks. A green or lighter stop low in the ramp gives the ground specks, because the detail noise samples the ramp around the ground height.

The columns family draws round columns with a capital and a base in front of another structure, for temples and halls. It has count (the integer number of columns per tile, spaced evenly so that no column touches a vertical tile edge), width (the shaft width as a fraction of the tile, capped so that the widest part of a column stays inside its slot), capital and base (the heights of the capital and base zones as fractions of the tile, where 0 removes them; the outer 35 percent of each zone is the flat abacus or plinth and the rest flares into the shaft), flare (from 0 to 1, how much wider the capital and base are than the shaft), flutes (the integer number of flutes across the shaft, where 0 is a smooth shaft) and behind, which is a full structure object of any other family drawn between the columns. The parameters of the structure behind appear in settings.json, in the parameter sets and in the GUI under the path behind, for example base.structure.behind.rows, with the label prefix Behind, and the family behind the columns is set in the preset file because the GUI has no selector for it. The structure behind takes the ramp from t 0 to 0.45 and the columns the band from t 0.55 to 1, with the shaft at 1 on its axis falling toward 0.55 at its silhouette and the abacus and plinth flat near 0.92, so a preset puts the wall colors low and the marble colors high and keeps the detail strength under about 0.2 so that the grain does not cross the gap. A column runs from the top edge to the bottom edge of the tile, so every field of this family is fixed and the columns are identical in every variant of a set (DECISIONS.md ADR-016), and the wall next to a column darkens through edgeDarkening like a shadow.

The crystal family draws clusters of pointed crystal shards radiating from scattered centers, for crystal caves, as a base or as a transparent garbage over rock. It has count (the integer number of lattice cells per tile, with at most one cluster per cell), density (from 0 to 1, the share of cells that hold a cluster), shards (the integer largest number of shards in a cluster, from 1 to 6, where every cluster has at least two when it may), size (the shard length in cells, from 0.1 to 1, capped so that a cluster stays inside the neighborhood of its cell), aspect (the shard width as a share of its length, from 0.05 to 1), sizeVariation (from 0 to 1, where 1 moves a length by up to 60 percent either way) and facet (from 0 to 1, how much darker the side faces are than the flat top face, with one side darker than the other per shard). Every cluster grows out of a soft dark knob that takes the ramp's bottom color, and the ground between clusters is the gap of this family, so a transparent garbage shows the base there. In a cluster that reaches a tile edge the center, the knob and the shards that reach the edge are shared by every variant of a set and use the authored density, size, aspect and sizeVariation, while its other shards and every cluster inside the tile come from the variant (DECISIONS.md ADR-018 and ADR-019), so those four may range, while count, shards and facet never range. Use cellColors with a banded ramp, where each color holds for a stretch of t, so that every shard is one clear color. For bigger crystals lower the count rather than raising the size past its cap, and stop at five cells, because at four cells nearly every cluster reaches an edge and a room repeats the centers and the crossing shards in every tile.

## Worked example

The lava cave preset uses the cells family for the floor base with a dark gray ramp, so that the floor reads as cooled rock crust, and it uses the field family for the garbage material with ridged noise and a ramp from near black through dark red to bright orange, so that garbage reads as glowing lava breaking through the crust. The client asked for the red to be the garbage, because the slider then adds lava to a walkable floor instead of adding crust to a lava lake. The wall base is the field family without ridges and a dark basalt ramp, and the wall garbage is a ridged lava field, so that garbage on walls reads as glowing veins. The variants count is 4 and jitter is 0.1, and the ranged parameters are the lava field scale, the warp strength, the lava detail strength and the floor's garbage scale, so that variants differ in the size and density of the lava cracks without changing the palette.

The cursed lands preset shows two uses of the cells family that are not stones. Its floor garbage is a cells material whose ramp stays at the dirt color up to t 0.74 and turns bone white only above t 0.9, so only the top of each dome shows and a garbage patch reads as bone bits scattered on dead ground instead of as a pavement. A small cell shows no bone at all and a large one shows a blob, which is where the scatter comes from, and the dirt color matches the mid tone of the base so that the patch does not read as a different ground. The ice cave wall is a cells material with contrast 1.5 and a tint of 0.4, so the dome flattens into one level per cell and the per-cell tint turns the cells into facets at different brightness, which reads as ice blocks or crystal faces. Use a thin edgeWidth (0.006 to 0.012) and a border color close to the interior for both, because a wide dark border turns any cells material into a net.

The dungeon preset is the worked example of the blocks family. Its wall is brown fieldstone with five courses, about four blocks per course, a size variation of 0.7 so that small stones fill the gaps beside large ones, a tall chance of 0.2, a corner radius of 0.02 and an edge darkening of 0.5, which together read as rounded stones in dark mortar. Its floor is gray flagstones with four courses and a tall chance of 0.3. The garbage is a field material in moss green on the floor and in lichen yellow on the wall, so that the slider adds growth to the joints.

The medieval preset is the worked example of the timber family. Its wall has two by two plaster panels in a frame of beam width 0.08 with pegs, a brace in about half of the panels, grain 0.6 and an edge darkening of 0.35 for the shadow along the frame, over a ramp that runs from dark wood through light wood into sand colored plaster. Its floor is the same family without a frame and with planks of width 0.1, so it is a plank floor with staggered ends and knots, and its garbage is straw. Dark mold is the garbage on the wall.

The swamp preset is the worked example of the scatter family. Its floor has six cells per tile with mounds of radius 0.55 cells, a size variation of 0.6, a second layer of pebbles, full jitter and a warp of 0.4, over a ramp that runs from dark mud through a green stop at t 0.07 into brown mound tops, so the gaps show mud with green specks and the mounds a dark rim. Its wall is the same family with four cells of wet gray boulders. The garbage on both is a green field material that reads as moss and algae.

The temple preset is the worked example of the columns family. Its wall has two fluted columns of width 0.14 with a capital zone of 0.14 and a base zone of 0.09 in front of a blocks structure of dark ashlar, over a ramp whose lower band runs from near black mortar to dark gray stone and whose upper band runs from shadowed to bright marble, with ivy as the garbage. Its floor is the pattern family as pale marble slabs with dust as the garbage.

The enchanted preset is the worked example of a transparent garbage. Its fairy dust is a scatter material with 24 cells, three layers, a density of 0.35, small stones with a size variation of 0.8 and a star share of 0.18, with cellColors over a ramp that runs from a magenta glow through pink, violet, blue, mint and gold to white, at a transparency of 0.8, so that the sparkles take their own colors, fade into the floor at their rims, and leave a faint magenta haze where the dust lies. The mask softness of 0.3 fades the dust region out slowly. The floor is dark slate slabs and the wall dark stone cells.

The magic cave preset shows cellColors on a cells material and a ridged field in glowing colors. Its wall is the cells family with a thin edge, a contrast of 1.6 and cellColors over a ramp of violet, magenta, teal and blue, so every cell is a flat crystal facet in its own color with a dark rim from edgeDarkening. Its floor is a ridged, warped field whose ramp runs from near black purple through indigo and violet into cyan and white at the top, so the ridges become glowing veins through dark rock. Both carry the dust of the enchanted preset in pink, gold, mint and blue at a transparency of 0.75.

The crystal cave preset is the worked example of the crystal family in both roles. Its wall is a crystal base with five cells, up to six shards per cluster, a size of 0.85 cells, an aspect of 0.45 and a facet of 0.9, with cellColors over a banded ramp of cyan, violet, magenta, mint and white on a near black ground, so the wall is a dense field of colored prisms. Its floor is a dark blue rock field with crystal clusters as a garbage at transparency 1, with five cells, up to four shards per cluster, a size of 0.85 cells and an aspect of 0.7, so a few fat clusters grow whole out of the rock wherever the mask covers their centers and still read at game scale, and the wall carries the dust of the enchanted preset.

The dozen high fantasy samples show the families in combination and are meant to be copied. Tavern is planks under half-timbered plaster with straw and soot. Forgotten castle is mossy ashlar in both roles. Wizard tower is violet marble with gold joints under a trail of cyan and gold sparkles, with blue rune cracks on the wall. Elven hall is forest floor with scattered leaves under a wall of planks between pale columns. Dwarven forge is dark iron blocks with embers and lava veins. Catacombs is a flagstone floor with scattered bones under a wall heaped from skulls, where the wall is the scatter family at full density with cellColors over a banded bone ramp. Throne room is white marble with gold joints and rose petals under fluted columns on sandstone with gold dust. Dragon's lair is dark rock under a hoard of gold coins, where the coins are a scatter garbage at transparency 1 with a banded gold ramp and a strong edge darkening. Haunted library is dark planks and shelving with cobweb haze. Frozen fortress is ice blocks with snow and frost crystals. Sunken temple is teal flagstones under water haze with columns on mossy ashlar. Mushroom grotto is cave floor with red and amber caps under a rock wall with glowing spores.

One lesson from the samples: a scatter material with a jitter under about 0.8 keeps its stones near the cell centers, so its ground gaps fall on the lattice lines, and the tile edge is a lattice line. The tiles still wrap, but the tileability test compares the seam pair against ordinary interior pairs and flags the gaps as a seam. Keep jitter at 0.9 or higher in a dense scatter, which also hides the lattice.

## Adding a preset

Copy the closest existing preset, change id and displayName, edit the ramps first because color carries most of the look, then adjust the structure, and render a contact sheet with the CLI before touching the detail parameters. A preset that renders a visible seam, or whose measured garbage coverage is off by more than 0.01, fails the tests and is not shipped.
